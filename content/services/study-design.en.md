faq:
  - q: "Is this really necessary for a small study?"
    a: |
      Small studies are exactly where it matters most. With a large sample, an
      approximate method often remains usable; with thirty subjects, poor sizing
      condemns the study to conclude nothing at all. The result is then not
      "negative", it is **uninterpretable** — which is far worse.
  - q: "I have no idea what effect to expect. How can a sample size be calculated?"
    a: |
      That is the normal situation and it is not a blocker. We work in scenarios:
      what is the smallest effect that would genuinely matter to you? We size on
      that value, and I show you how the number of subjects moves with the
      assumptions. You then choose knowing the trade-off between recruitment cost
      and the risk of detecting nothing.
  - q: "Collection has already started. Is it too late?"
    a: |
      Not necessarily. Depending on how far along you are, it is often still
      possible to adjust recruitment, add a variable or fix the analysis plan. Get
      in touch: I will tell you frankly what can still be salvaged and what cannot.
      If analysis is your real need, [data analysis](/en/services/data-analysis/)
      or [support for researchers](/en/services/research-support/) is the better
      fit.
  - q: "Will the ethics committee accept the calculation?"
    a: |
      A sample size calculation is accepted when its assumptions are explicit and
      justified: expected effect and its source, standard deviation, significance
      level, target power, planned test. That is exactly what the deliverable
      contains, written to go straight into your application.
  - q: "How long does it take?"
